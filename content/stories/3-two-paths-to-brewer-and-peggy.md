[[Brewer McKeehan (1770)|Brewer McKeehan]] was born about 1770 in Cumberland County, Pennsylvania, the same county where Mary McKeehan Patton made powder before the Revolution. On 27 March 1794 he married her daughter, [[Margaret Patton (1773)|Margaret "Peggy" Patton]]. Because Brewer and Mary were both McKeehans from Cumberland County, many people assume they were related, perhaps aunt and nephew, but no document seen so far says so.

Two of Brewer and Peggy's sons matter to this tree.

- [[Samuel McKeehan (1795)|Samuel "Brewer" McKeehan]], born about 1795, married [[Isabella Simerly (1793)|Isabella Simerly]] in 1819. Family tradition says he was killed by gas fumes in a cave while fox hunting.
- [[George McKeehan (1802)|George McKeehan]], born in 1802 in Carter County, married [[Hannah Lusk (1806)|Hannah Lusk]] in 1824.

George's son [[Samuel W. McKeehan (1832)|Samuel W. McKeehan]] was born on 15 April 1832. Samuel Brewer's daughter [[Hannah Elizabeth McKeehan (1836)|Hannah Elizabeth McKeehan]] was born on 11 December 1836, a twin of [[Caroline McKeehan (1836)|Caroline]]. The two were first cousins, and on 10 May 1860 they married.

Because of that marriage, every one of their descendants, Dawn included, reaches Brewer and Peggy along two separate lines, and Mary McKeehan Patton along two lines as well. Genealogists call this pedigree collapse. On the Ancestors chart you can see it: Brewer and Peggy fill two slots in the sixth ring, and John and Mary Patton fill two in the seventh.

Samuel W. died in Tennessee on 31 March 1890. Hannah Elizabeth went west with her children in 1898 and died in Pullman, Washington, on 16 April 1912. She is buried in the Hatley Cemetery at Union Flat.

**Still to check:** Find a Grave lists her as Hannah Elizabeth Douglas Dodge, born 1838. A maiden name of Douglas would contradict the family records that make her Samuel Brewer's daughter, so that question is open.
