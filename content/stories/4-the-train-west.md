[[Samuel Rosencrans McKeehan (1864)|Samuel Rosencrans McKeehan]] was born on 22 February 1864 and grew up on Toll Branch, between Milligan College and Powder Branch in Carter County, Tennessee, close to the old Patton powder mill. On 15 April 1887 he married [[Julia Ann Hatley (1866)|Julia Ann Hatley]], whose family lived in Butler.

![Anna (Ford) Hatley](media/william_hatley_and_anna_ford.jpg "Julia's grandmother [[Anna Ford (1813)|Anna (Ford) Hatley]]. The other half of this old photograph, which showed her husband [[William M. Hatley (1807)|William]], has faded away.")
![Amos and Julia Greene](media/amos_and_julia_greene.jpg "Julia's other grandparents, [[Amos Greene]] and [[Julia Elizabeth McCandless]].")

Samuel had seen the West before he moved there. Courthouse papers in Colfax, Washington, name him in July 1893 as a witness in a case brought against his "Uncle Riley," whose son-in-law was trying to have him declared insane. (Riley was found sane.)

![The Whitman County courthouse at Colfax](media/stories/colfax-courthouse-1895.jpg "The Whitman County courthouse at Colfax, about 1895, close to the time Samuel was a witness there.")

In 1898 Samuel and Julia went west by train with their children and everything they owned. Samuel's brother [[Thomas Edward McKeehan (1866)|Edward]] and his sisters [[Lucy Belle McKeehan (1870)|Belle]] and [[Margaret Hannah Elizabeth McKeehan (1873)|Margaret]] came too, with their families, and so did his widowed mother, [[Hannah Elizabeth McKeehan (1836)|Hannah Elizabeth]]. Among the children on the train were [[Nathaniel Smith McKeehan (1888)|Smith]], [[Nora Martisha McKeehan (1893)|Nora]] and the baby, [[Ben LeRoy McKeehan (1897)|Ben]].

By then the northern railroads, finished in the 1880s, had opened the Palouse to settlers, and its rolling hills were becoming some of the richest wheat country in the world.

![An 1893 Northern Pacific Railroad advertisement](media/stories/np-map-1893.jpg "A page from an 1893 Northern Pacific Railroad booklet, advertising dining cars and Pullman sleeping cars on the trains west.")

![Wheat harvest on the Palouse, 1908](media/stories/palouse-harvest-1908.jpg "Harvest on the Palouse in 1908. The postcard boasts that a combined harvester like this one, pulled by a big team of horses, could cut, thresh and sack 20 acres of wheat in a day.")

They lived first south of Pullman, where [[Ralph William McKeehan (1899)|Ralph]] was born in 1899. Around 1900 they took up a homestead near Hartline, in what is now Grant County, and the children went to the Timm School north of Wilson Creek. [[Edna Kae McKeehan (1903)|Edna]] was born on that homestead in 1903. After a stay at Waterville, the family moved to a homestead in the Big Meadow Creek country near Troy, Idaho, where [[Russell Roosevelt McKeehan (1905)|Russell]] and [[Mack Merle McKeehan (1907)|Mack]] were born.

![Troy, Idaho, about 1907](media/stories/troy-1907.jpg "Troy, Idaho, about 1907. The town had been called Vollmer until 1897.")

Julia died at North Troy on 30 May 1949, and Samuel followed her there on 3 September. He is buried in the Moscow Cemetery.
