[[Isabella Simerly (1793)|Isabella Simerly]], called Ibby, was born on 14 July 1793 in Washington County, which was then the far western edge of North Carolina and is now East Tennessee. On 30 December 1819 she married [[Samuel McKeehan (1795)|Samuel "Brewer" McKeehan]]. He died about 1836; she lived almost sixty years longer, and if the dates are right she died on 13 July 1895, one day short of her 102nd birthday. She is buried in the McKeehan Cemetery on Toll Branch Road in Carter County.

The family papers name her father simply as Adam Simmerly, and her mother as [[Hannah Nowland (1763)|Hannah Nowland]], who may have been born in Marblehead, Massachusetts, in 1763. Adam died in Carter County on 5 March 1833.

### The German lead

Online family trees go much further. They make Adam into [[Johann Adam Zimmerle (1755)|Johann Adam Zimmerle]], born on 26 September 1755 in Altdorf, a village in the Palatinate wine country west of the Rhine. His parents in those trees are [[Johannes Zimmerle (1726)|Johannes Zimmerle]] and [[Maria Apollonia Vester (1727)|Maria Apollonia Vester]], both born in Altdorf. Maria Apollonia died there in 1761, and Johannes is said to have brought the family to the German settlements of the South Carolina backcountry, dying at Irmo in the Dutch Fork on 14 May 1789.

The story fits a well-known pattern: thousands of German-speaking "Palatines" left the Rhineland for British America in the 1700s, and English-speaking clerks often wrote Zimmerle as Simerly. But none of it has been checked against original records yet, so on this site the German generations are shown as a lead, not a fact.
