[[Halvor Petar Shodene (1861)|Halvor Petar Shodene]] was born on 10 June 1861 at Östgården, a farm in the village of Linsell in Härjedalen, the province in west-central Sweden that is now part of Jämtland County. He was born Halvar Persson. His father, [[Per Halvarsson (1826)|Per Halvarsson]], was the first farmer at Östgården, and his mother was [[Karin Olofsdotter (1823)|Karin Olofsdotter]]. The family group record writes his name as "Halver Petar (Petersen) Shodene" with *Sjödin* written above it. In America he used the surname Sjödin, which became Shodene; the family says a schoolteacher changed the spelling when the children started school. His ancestors are traced back to the 1500s in *Twelve generations in Härjedalen*.

[[Märet Zakrisdotter (1869)|Märet Zakrisdotter]] was born on 5 January 1869 in the same village of Linsell. Her name follows the old Swedish custom: she was Zakri's daughter, and her father, [[Zakri Zakrisson]], was the son of another Zakri. Her mother, [[Maret Mattsdotter]], died at Linsell in 1895, and when the farm was divided in January 1897 Märet inherited a share of it, even though she had long since left for America. She left with a *flyttningsbevis*, the moving certificate that a Swedish parish gave anyone moving away, made out to Märeth Sakrisdotter of Linsell, bound for North America, on 19 September of 1891 or 1892.

Halvor and Märet were part of the great Swedish emigration of the late 1800s, when more than a million Swedes left for America. Most of them sailed from Gothenburg, usually by way of an English port such as Hull or Liverpool, on tickets sold by agents for the steamship lines.

![Emigrant agents in Gothenburg](media/stories/gothenburg-agents.jpg "Agents outside the White Star Line's office on Sillgatan in Gothenburg, sometime between 1860 and 1895.")

They married in Moscow, Idaho, on 26 November 1892 and raised seven children near Troy. In America Märet was known as Mary Shodene. The family homesteaded 160 acres in the White Pine country, about four miles northeast of Troy.

Their daughter [[Elsie Eleanor Shodene (1903)|Elsie]] was born in Troy on 9 October 1903. On 26 December 1928, at Clarkston, Washington, she married [[Ben LeRoy McKeehan (1897)|Ben McKeehan]], who had come west from Tennessee on the train as a baby and had served in France and Germany in the First World War.

![Ben McKeehan in uniform](media/ben_mckeehan_wwi.jpg "[[Ben LeRoy McKeehan (1897)|Ben McKeehan]] in his World War I uniform.")

Ben worked for Washington Water Power and the Grant County PUD, and they raised nine children in Grant County, all of whom graduated from Wilson Creek High School.

![Grand Coulee Dam under construction, 1938](media/stories/grand-coulee-1938.jpg "Grand Coulee Dam under construction in 1938. The dam brought power, and from the 1950s irrigation water, to the Columbia Basin where Ben and Elsie raised their family.")

Märet died at Troy on 19 August 1934, and Halvor on 20 June 1946. The Shodene papers place his death at Ephrata, where Elsie lived, but the family record in *More Swedish Genealogy* and FamilySearch place it at Troy. Both are buried in the Burnt Ridge Cemetery near Troy. Elsie died in Ephrata on 27 January 1969 (some family papers say the 28th).
