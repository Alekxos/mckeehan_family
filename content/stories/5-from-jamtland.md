[[Halvor Petar Shodene (1861)|Halvor Petar Shodene]] was born on 10 June 1861 in Jämtland, in north-central Sweden. The family group record writes his name as "Halver Petar (Petersen) Shodene" with *Sjödin* written above it, so Shodene is probably the American spelling of the Swedish surname Sjödin, and his father was probably named Peter.

[[Märet Zakrisdotter (1869)|Märet Zakrisdotter]] was born on 5 January 1869 at Linsell, a farming village in Härjedalen. Her name follows the old Swedish custom: she was Zakri's daughter, and her father, [[Zakri Zakrisson]], was the son of another Zakri. Her mother, [[Maret Mattsdotter]], died at Linsell in 1895, and when the farm was divided in January 1897 Märet inherited a share of it, even though she had long since left for America.

![Zakri Zakrisson and Maret Mattsdotter](media/zakri_zakrisson_and_maret_mattsdotter.jpg "Märet's parents, [[Zakri Zakrisson]] and [[Maret Mattsdotter]]. The handwritten note at the top mentions “Maret Zakrisson (Mary Shodene)”.")
![The church at Linsell](media/stories/linsell-church.jpg "The church at Linsell, Märet's home parish in Härjedalen.")

Halvor and Märet were part of the great Swedish emigration of the late 1800s, when more than a million Swedes left for America. Most of them sailed from Gothenburg, usually by way of an English port such as Hull or Liverpool, on tickets sold by agents for the steamship lines.

![Emigrant agents in Gothenburg](media/stories/gothenburg-agents.jpg "Agents outside the White Star Line's office on Sillgatan in Gothenburg, sometime between 1860 and 1895.")

 They married in Moscow, Idaho, on 26 November 1892 and raised seven children near Troy. In America Märet was known as Mary Shodene.

![Main Street, Troy, Idaho, in winter 1913](media/stories/troy-main-1913.jpg "Main Street in Troy in the winter of 1913, when the Shodene children were growing up nearby.")

Their daughter [[Elsie Eleanor Shodene (1903)|Elsie]] was born in Troy on 9 October 1903. On 24 December 1928 she married [[Ben LeRoy McKeehan (1897)|Ben McKeehan]], who had come west from Tennessee on the train as a baby and had served in France and Germany in the First World War.

![Ben McKeehan in uniform](media/ben_mckeehan_wwi.jpg "[[Ben LeRoy McKeehan (1897)|Ben McKeehan]] in his World War I uniform.")

Ben worked for Washington Water Power and the Grant County PUD, and they raised nine children in Grant County, all of whom graduated from Wilson Creek High School.

![Grand Coulee Dam under construction, 1938](media/stories/grand-coulee-1938.jpg "Grand Coulee Dam under construction in 1938. The dam brought power, and from the 1950s irrigation water, to the Columbia Basin where Ben and Elsie raised their family.")

Märet died in 1934 and Halvor in Ephrata in 1946. Both are buried in the Burnt Ridge Cemetery near Troy. Elsie died in Ephrata on 28 January 1969.
